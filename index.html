Travel Checklist — Dubai → Guangzhou → Hanoi → Hong Kong → Chengdu → Dubai

A practical, actionable checklist for a female solo traveler. Focus: electronics, money, female period care, food safety, transport and safety tips. (Visa matters excluded.)

Itinerary (quick view)

Departure: Dubai (night) → Arrive Guangzhou (07:00) → transfer to Hanoi → Stay in Hanoi until 7th → Fly to Hong Kong (period starts on 8th in Hong Kong) → 12th depart Hong Kong → flight via Chengdu Tianfu → Return to Dubai.

1. Plugs, Voltage & Charging

RegionPlug TypeNotes
DubaiType G (UK three-pin)220–240V — same as Hong Kong. Bring a UK/Type-G adapter if your plugs differ.
China (Guangzhou, Chengdu)Type A/I (Chinese standard)220V — bring a universal adapter or a China plug adapter.
Vietnam (Hanoi)Type C/F (European two-pin common)220V — many sockets accept multiple pin types. Use a small universal adapter.
Hong KongType G (UK three-pin)220–240V — same as Dubai.
  • Must bring: a universal travel adapter that supports Type G/C/F/A/I.
  • Bring a multi-port USB power strip (with surge protection) to charge phone, power bank and other devices at once.
  • Carry extra charging cables and a power bank rated <20000mAh (airline-friendly).

2. Mobile Data & Connectivity

  • Best option: buy a multi-country eSIM or physical SIM covering China, Vietnam and Hong Kong. Confirm it supports hotspot tethering.
  • Alternative: buy a separate local SIM after arrival in Hanoi and top up; airport SIMs are convenient but usually pricier.
  • Save important files offline: hotel addresses, emergency contacts, and digital copies of passport & travel documents.

3. Money & Cards

  • Bring one international credit/debit card (Visa or MasterCard).
  • Keep cash split across two places (wallet + hotel safe/backpack).
  • Avoid changing large amounts at airports — check local exchange rates before travelling.

4. Female Period & Health (period starts on 8th while in Hong Kong)

Pack in a small personal pouch: sanitary pads or tampons, panty liners, painkillers (ibuprofen or paracetamol), heating patches/warmers, wet wipes, spare underwear, and a small zip bag for used items.
  • Bring sufficient sanitary supplies for the whole trip plus extras — Hong Kong & Vietnam sell them, but carrying your preferred brand is more comfortable.
  • Pack a small first-aid kit: painkillers, anti-diarrheal medicine, antacid, motion-sickness tablets and any personal prescriptions.
  • Avoid excessive spicy food and very cold drinks during the first 1–2 days of the period; get rest and stay hydrated.

5. Food & Drinking Safety

  • In Vietnam, avoid ice from unknown sources and raw street-side seafood; prefer bottled water.
  • Use hand sanitizer and wet wipes before eating street food.
  • Carry simple stomach remedies: oral rehydration salts, loperamide, and an antacid.

6. Transport Tips (free-style travel)

  • Use ride apps: Grab is recommended in Vietnam; use local apps (Uber/DiDi) where available. For Hong Kong, use MTR + Octopus card.
  • At airports, take official taxis or app-ordered cars; avoid random touts offering rides.
  • Take photos of taxi plates/driver and share live location with a friend when taking late rides.

7. Safety & Practical Tips

  • Keep digital and physical copies of passport, ID, flight tickets, and hotel bookings (one cloud + one offline copy).
  • Split money & cards between your wallet and a hidden pouch/backpack.
  • Be cautious with strangers offering unsolicited help; don’t hand over your phone or passport to anyone except official staff.
  • If something is lost or stolen, contact local police and your embassy/consulate immediately.

  • Save local emergency numbers and the contact details of your embassy/consulate.
  • Make a note of the hotel addresses in local language to show to taxi drivers.
  • Share your itinerary with a trusted friend and enable location-sharing on your phone while travelling.
